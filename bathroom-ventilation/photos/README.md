## What the photograph shows

The fan's intake faces the room, with its grille removed. Metal mounting rails connect it to exposed wood framing below a painted plank ceiling. A short, foil-taped metal duct extends from the fan's side and appears to turn into the ceiling at its far end. The exterior termination and roof assembly are not visible.

This supports considering one elongated indoor enclosure around both the fan and the visible duct. The existing framing may relate to the contractor's proposed dropped ceiling, but the photograph does not establish whether individual framing members could be removed or incorporated into a smaller enclosure.

The fan body appears to occupy space below the wood ceiling. The photo does not establish how much, if any, is recessed above it. It also does not reveal the previously discussed ceiling opening around or behind the fan. The visible duct penetration is a separate feature; do not infer the dimensions of either opening from this view.

## Implications for the enclosure proposal

Measure the distance from the original wood ceiling to the fan's grille mounting plane and to the lowest part of the duct/elbow. Those measurements, plus framing and finish allowances, determine the actual enclosure drop. A shallower replacement fan only reduces the completed enclosure depth if the duct and elbow can fit within that smaller depth too.

A second matching grille could conceal access beneath the duct section. The image shows that section on the fan's discharge side: a spare grille opening into the surrounding enclosure would not, by itself, connect that cavity to the fan's intake. Passive exchange and deliberate exhaust airflow remain different design cases.

This is a visual assessment, not a measured installation drawing or confirmation of hidden conditions, support, duct sealing, airflow, electrical work or roof weatherproofing. The earlier conceptual enclosure dimensions remain provisional.
