# Ceiling grilles for Fantech RE54 / REC54

Researched September 22, 2026. Follow-up to the [roof ventilation comparison](README.md). The tongue-and-groove ceiling is directly beneath the roof, with no attic. The existing ceiling opening has not been measured.

**Yes: these roof fans can draw through a separate passive grille at the shower ceiling.** The grille contains no motor or light. A sealed duct connects it to the fan outdoors. Fantech's [RE/REC installation manual](spec-sheets/Fantech-RE-REC-installation.pdf), p. 3, illustrates a ceiling grille beneath a roof installation; its [bathroom design guidance](https://www.fantech.net/en-us/our-solutions/residential/bathroom-ventilation-options/home-spas) also places exhaust pickup points at showers and other moisture sources.

The basic arrangement is **shower ceiling grille → sealed transition/duct → roof-mounted fan → outdoors**. For this house, any space needed for the transition and duct must be accommodated through/above the roof in the roofer's weatherproof detail. The grille alone does not establish the roof assembly or the fan's code compliance.

## Which I would discuss with the installer

**For retaining the existing square opening, start with the RH45 plus a fabricated sealed transition and mounting frame.** This should give a conventional square grille appearance and avoids having a small round valve in a much larger blank plate. The aluminum face is a useful material choice for a humid bathroom. This recommendation is based on geometry and material, not a verified airflow calculation for the finished assembly.

The Panasonic manual specifies a **10½ × 10½ ceiling cutout for new construction** (p. 9), and **10⅞ × 10⅞ for retrofit** (p. 10); neither is a measurement of the contractor's hole. If the actual hole is 10½ in. square, the RH45 face overlaps it by **⅝ in. on each edge** when centered. Crucially, its drawing places the mounting screws **⅝ in. inward from the face edges**: those screw centers would coincide with the cutout edges. **Provide a mounting frame/backing rather than trying to screw into the thin edge of the cut decking.** The submittal calls for a duct opening equal to nominal size ±⅛ in.; the frame/transition must provide that interface. The face can conceal the larger hole, but concealment alone does not establish a proper fit.

For a compact round appearance, the CG5 or MGE5 is simpler to connect to nominal 5-in. duct, but the wood repair or cover plate will be visible. A 13-in. plate is a suggested starting size, matching the ordered Panasonic's grille footprint; the installer must size it from actual measurements. It is finish trim, not a structural repair to roof decking.

The CG5 uses the smaller CG45 grille body with a 5-in. mounting collar. A 5-in. collar does not mean the entire air passage is 5 in. wide. Its manufacturer sheet includes pressure/noise curves; the selected opening setting and delivered airflow need checking. The grille should remain removable for cleaning. Do not add an HVAC filter to this bathroom exhaust inlet.

Use a continuous sealed air path to the outdoors, with backdraft protection and condensation/insulation details included in the roof design. Resolve the fan certification and short-duct sound questions already described in the [main report](README.md#california-and-contra-costa-checks-that-affect-selection). A passive grille over the shower does not, by itself, answer those system questions.
