# Shower Pans — Terrazzo Look + Custom Sizing

Researched August 8, 2026. Kicked off by the Ferguson product Duncan shared.

## The product you sent

**Transolid FPT6032L-HO Pre-Tiled Rectangular Shower Base** — 60" × 32-1/4", single threshold, **left** drain, "Off-White Hexagon" finish. **$1,753.34.** Model FPT6032L-HO · Item bci4819001.
Link: https://www.fergusonhome.com/product/summary/2017269

What it is: a one-piece pre-tiled base with a marble-hexagon / terrazzo-like surface, so the shower floor is finished at the factory — no floor tiling, mud bed, or floor waterproofing needed. On Ferguson it shows three finish options and comes in a handful of stock sizes (60×32 and 60×36) with left / right / center drain versions.

The catch for your case: **it is not custom-sized.** You pick from stock dimensions. It's a great low-labor choice *only if* your shower opening is very close to 60×32 or 60×36 and the drain lands where the model puts it. If your opening is an odd Eichler dimension, look at the custom options below.

## Getting a terrazzo look at YOUR exact size — options, best fit first

### 1. Custom tileable pan + real terrazzo (truest terrazzo, any size)
Order a made-to-measure *tileable* base, then finish its floor with actual terrazzo tile or terrazzo-look porcelain. This is the only route that gives a genuine terrazzo aggregate at an exact size.
- **Tile Redi "Redi Your Way"** — built to your exact width and depth, drain placed where you measure, curbed **or** barrier-free (curbless). Ships with an integrated drain; you add their waterproofing and your finish tile. ~4–5 week lead. https://tileredi.com/products/custom-shower-pans
- **Transolid Ready-to-Tile / Ready Tile bases** — cheaper sloped-and-drained bases you tile yourself (mostly near-stock sizes, e.g. 60×32 at ~$919). Good if a stock-ish size works and you just want to choose the tile.
- Pair either with terrazzo tile (Fireclay, Concrete Collaborative, Clé) or terrazzo-look porcelain, which is lighter and easier to install on an Eichler slab than real poured/precast terrazzo.

### 2. The Onyx Collection — custom-size solid-surface base (grout-free)
Made to order to your exact dimensions, any drain location, curbed or barrier-free/ADA, in solid surface (no floor grout to maintain). This is the champion for *custom size + low maintenance*.
- Honest caveat: Onyx colors are mostly solid or **subtly** speckled — a few "granite" looks lean toward aggregate, but it is **not** a bold terrazzo. Choose Onyx if custom size and a grout-free surface matter more than a strong terrazzo pattern; choose option 1 if the terrazzo look is the priority.
- Custom base info: https://onyxcollection.com/custom-shower-base-pan-full-height-curb/ · Colors: https://onyxcollection.com/colors/

### 3. Cultured-marble custom pans (regional fabricators)
Cultured marble is poured to any size and can be made with heavy speckle that reads much closer to terrazzo than Onyx. Sourced from local shops rather than nationally — worth a Bay Area cultured-marble fabricator quote.
- Example vendor (illustrates the product type): https://www.marblelite.com/shower-pans

### 4. One-piece custom terrazzo or terrazzo solid-surface base  ← **now the leading option**

> **Correction (2026-08-09):** an earlier version of this note said real terrazzo pans came "mostly in fixed commercial sizes" and dismissed them. That was wrong. Several fabricators make **genuinely custom, one-piece terrazzo shower bases** with the slope cast in at the factory. See **`custom-terrazzo-shower-bases.md`** for the verified vendors, the epoxy-vs-cement question, and the pre-order checklist.

Short version: **Angelozzi TERRALITE** (100% epoxy terrazzo, custom sizes/shapes, linear "infinity" drains cast directly in), **Creative Industries** (precast cement terrazzo, any size/any color, five threshold styles), and **Grifform** (custom Corian/HI-MACS pans in terrazzo-look colors, slip-resistant finish available). A one-piece base gives the monolithic mid-century look with **no grout joints at all** — arguably more architecturally correct for an Eichler than tile.

- Historical background: https://retrorenovation.com/2016/02/22/terrazzo-shower-bases-fiat/

## Practical notes for the Eichler

- **Measure first:** exact opening width × depth, plus the existing drain location (distance from back wall and from a side wall). On a slab-on-grade Eichler, moving the drain means opening the slab, so favor a pan whose drain matches your current plumbing.
- **Curbless?** A barrier-free pan needs the slab recessed — decide that before you order, since it drives demo and framing.
- **Weight/handling:** terrazzo-look porcelain over a tileable pan is far lighter and more forgiving on a slab than real terrazzo.
- **Matching the vibe:** the terrazzo direction pairs naturally with the walnut-vanity-plus-one-color scheme in the inspiration board — a soft terrazzo floor reads mid-century without competing with a green or blue tile wall.
