# Real Terrazzo Tile in the Shower Pan — Yes, With Conditions

Researched August 8, 2026. Answers "could I use real terrazzo tiles in the shower pan?"

**Short answer:** Yes, you can tile a shower pan with real terrazzo tile. Three things need managing — finish/slip, format vs. the slope, and sealing. Handle those and it works well; ignore them and you get a floor that's slippery and stains.

## Real terrazzo tile vs. terrazzo-look porcelain

- **Real terrazzo** = marble/quartz/glass chips in a **cement or epoxy** binder, ground and polished. Authentic depth; can be honed for grip. Cement-based terrazzo is **porous and needs sealing**; epoxy terrazzo is denser but is rarely sold as residential tile.
- **Terrazzo-look porcelain** = fired clay printed to look like terrazzo. **No sealing**, denser, lighter, cheaper, and more forgiving on uneven/sloped substrates. It looks like terrazzo rather than being it.

Both can last decades; porcelain resists cracking on uneven substrates better, and real terrazzo is generally considered less ideal for constantly-wet areas unless finished and sealed correctly.

## The three things to manage for a pan floor

1. **Finish & slip.** Polished terrazzo is slippery when wet. Use a **honed/matte or lightly textured (sandblasted)** finish. Targets: U.S. **ANSI A326.3 DCOF ≥ 0.42** (go higher for a shower); internationally, showers are often spec'd at **R11–R12 / barefoot-wet P4–P5**. Topical anti-slip sealers/additives can raise grip; ordinary impregnating sealers do not change slip.
2. **Format vs. the slope.** A shower pan slopes to the drain, so large rigid tiles can't sit flat. Use terrazzo in a **small format — mosaic or small squares — on the pan floor**, or keep large terrazzo tiles on the walls, curb, and bench and set a small-format terrazzo (or a slip-rated terrazzo-look porcelain mosaic) on the floor itself.
3. **Sealing & waterproofing.** Cement terrazzo is porous → seal at install and **reseal roughly once or twice a year** in a wet shower. Separately, the pan/substrate must have its **own waterproof membrane** — the tile is never the waterproofing layer.

## Weight & cost

Heavier and more expensive than porcelain, but an Eichler slab carries it easily; weight is mainly a handling and setting-flatness concern (watch for lippage on a sloped pan). Budget more than porcelain, and more labor if honing/sealing on site.

## Recommendation

- **Best of both worlds:** real terrazzo on the **walls / curb / bench** for authentic depth, plus a **small honed terrazzo mosaic** (or a slip-rated terrazzo-look porcelain mosaic) on the **pan floor**. You get the real material where slip doesn't matter and a safe, slope-friendly floor where it does.
- If you want **real terrazzo underfoot**, order it **honed (not polished), small format**, and commit to periodic resealing.
- Always get samples, **wet them and test barefoot**, and confirm the **DCOF / R rating for the exact finish** before buying.
