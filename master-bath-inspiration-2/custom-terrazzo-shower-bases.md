# Custom One-Piece Terrazzo Shower Bases

Researched and vendor-verified August 9, 2026. This supersedes the dismissive note about terrazzo receptors in `shower-pans-terrazzo.md`.

**The key point I had wrong:** custom, one-piece terrazzo shower bases are readily available for residential work. You are not limited to tiling a pan yourself. A cast base gives the monolithic, grout-free mid-century look — which is arguably *more* architecturally appropriate for an Eichler than a tiled floor, since it reads as a single plane rather than a grid.

There are two routes: genuine terrazzo, and terrazzo-pattern solid surface.

## Route 1 — Genuine terrazzo (cast aggregate)

Marble, stone, or glass chips cast in a binder and ground to expose the aggregate. Real depth and variation, not a printed pattern. The binder is the decisive choice.

### Epoxy vs. cement — pick epoxy

| | Epoxy terrazzo | Cement (portland) terrazzo |
|---|---|---|
| Porosity | Effectively non-porous | Porous — needs sealing and periodic resealing |
| Water resistance | Designed for wet use | Vendor-acknowledged: water can slowly erode and crack the cast |
| Weight | Lighter | Heavier |
| Cracking | More resistant | Can develop hairline cracks and edge damage |
| Character | Cleaner, more contemporary | Slightly more commercial/vintage |

For a shower — a permanently wet, hard-to-repair location — **epoxy is the better engineering choice.** Cement terrazzo is the more historically authentic material, so it's a legitimate pick if you want the vintage character and accept the sealing regime.

### Verified vendors

**Angelozzi TERRALITE** — https://angelozziterrazzo.com/terrazzo-shower-units/ · (856) 753-3200
100% epoxy terrazzo shower units. "Custom sizes and shapes available upon request." One-piece with the drainage slope molded into the cast. Notably, they can **cast a long rectangular linear/infinity drain directly into the unit**, and metal flashing for waterproofing is standard. They explicitly market epoxy against cement on the grounds that water erodes and cracks cement casts. **Start here** — it's the best match for a custom Eichler pan.

**Creative Industries Terrazzo** — https://www.creative-terrazzo.com/shower-bases.htm · (773) 235-9088
Precast terrazzo (crushed marble in portland cement), polished. "We can customize any size and any color," radius bases included. Five threshold configurations: single threshold, double threshold, neo corner, neo corner full flanges, full flanges. Drains are centered by default but **can be moved for a small charge** — relevant on a slab where your existing drain location is fixed. Cement-based, so factor in sealing.

## Route 2 — Terrazzo-pattern solid surface

Non-porous resin-based solid surface (Corian, HI-MACS) fabricated into a custom pan. The flecks are in the material, not printed on it, but it won't have the depth or irregularity of cast aggregate.

**Grifform Innovations** — https://www.grifform.com/products/custom/shower-pans · (541) 496-0313
Custom pans in DuPont Corian and LX Hausys HI-MACS. Terrazzo-look colors confirmed by name: **Domino Terrazzo, Laguna Terrazzo, Peppered Terrazzo**. 16 shape options, custom drain locations, straight-wall and no-drip-entry wall details. A **slip-resistant finish is an explicit add-on** — ask for it. All measurements are taken **stud to stud**, not finished wall. Their own caveat: some colors have random veining whose intensity varies between pieces, which can highlight seams.

Trade-offs: easiest to maintain, lightest, least crack-prone, and easy to coordinate with matching niches, benches, or a vanity top in the same material. But some patterns read more like granite or confetti than real terrazzo, and fabricated seams may be visible. Probably the safest balance of appearance, maintenance, and installation risk.

## Aesthetic direction for this house

A warm, light base — off-white or pale warm gray — with relatively **sparse** charcoal, rust, muted green, and cream aggregate. That reads mid-century rather than the busy "confetti terrazzo" currently in fashion. Pair with a matte or honed slip-resistant finish and a discreet linear or color-matched drain to keep it architectural. This also keeps the floor quiet enough not to fight a saturated green or blue tile wall.

## Confirm before ordering

Have the contractor verify all of these in writing:

1. **Exact stud-to-stud dimensions**, not finished-wall dimensions.
2. **Drain centerline**, and whether relocating the existing drain is feasible — on this slab-on-grade Eichler that may mean opening the slab near radiant lines, so prefer a base whose drain matches existing plumbing.
3. **Finished threshold height**, and whether you want curbless entry (curbless requires recessing the slab — decide early, it drives demo and framing).
4. **Weight and floor support requirements.**
5. **Tested slip-resistance rating** for the specific finish (target wet DCOF ≥ 0.42 per ANSI A326.3; higher is better in a shower).
6. **Integral wall flanges** and exactly how they tie into the wall waterproofing.
7. **Delivery route** — confirm the piece fits through every doorway and turn. A one-piece cast base cannot be trimmed on site.
8. **Who bears responsibility if field dimensions are wrong.** For a custom, non-returnable piece this is the single most expensive question, so settle it before the order goes in.

## Recommendation

For a custom base, favor **epoxy terrazzo (Angelozzi)** or **terrazzo-pattern solid surface (Grifform)** over cement terrazzo. Both keep the Eichler-appropriate appearance while reducing porosity, sealing, and cracking concerns. Keep the tiled-pan route in `shower-floor-terrazzo-porcelain.md` as the fallback if custom casting turns out to be too slow or too expensive.
