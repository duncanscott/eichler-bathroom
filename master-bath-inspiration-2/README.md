## What these references reinforce

The pins cluster tightly around the same directions the first collection identified, which is a good sign they're the real Eichler consensus:

- **Walnut/teak vanity + one saturated tile color.** Forest green and mid-century blue dominate the fresh pins (P01, P02, P03, P04, P09, P11). Green tile with warm wood is the single most repeated combination.
- **Keep the envelope calm.** The most convincing rooms pair the wood and color with plain white or warm-neutral everywhere else (P05, P06).
- **Open the wet zone.** Frameless glass, walk-in showers, tiled benches, and tubs pushed to the glazing recur (P07, P11, P12, P13).
- **Plan for a small footprint.** Several pins show tight three-fixture layouts that still feel resolved (P14, P15).

## Eichler-specific product & detail notes (from the web sources)

These are the concrete, sourced specifics worth carrying into a spec conversation:

- **Tile brands with genuine mid-century pedigree:** Heath Ceramics (Sausalito, founded 1948), Fireclay Tile (their midcentury collection and the *Destination Eichler* collaboration), and Modwalls "Brio" tiny squares for a retro look. Heath + Fireclay is exactly what Klopf Architecture used in the One Kindesign feature.
- **Tile size & setting:** Fireclay's *Honoring Eichler* project used **4x8 tile in Aqua set straight (not stacked)** as a deliberately period-honest install — a useful, specific alternative to the more contemporary stacked look. Small mosaics and 4x4 squares in soft aqua/mint/peach/gray also read authentic. Favor **matte or satin** finishes over glossy.
- **Fixtures:** chrome or brushed nickel, single-lever low-profile faucets, simple cylindrical knobs and straight pulls, frameless or minimally-framed glass. Consider an 18-inch-deep **wall-hung toilet** and a **floating vanity** to keep the small floor visually open.
- **Lighting:** a pair of **globe pendants** flanking a large frameless mirror is the signature move; lean on the skylight for daytime light and keep ceiling fixtures minimal. All fixtures rated for damp locations.
- **Slab / radiant-heat reality (reinforces the first folder's caution):** original master baths are often only ~40 sq ft; slab construction and post-and-beam framing make expansion and new duct/wiring runs hard, and failed radiant copper is commonly replaced with **electric heating mats**. Map and test radiant, water, and sewer before demolition.
