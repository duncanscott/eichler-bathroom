# Terrazzo-Look Porcelain for the Shower Floor (Slip-Safe)

Researched August 8, 2026. Follow-on to `shower-pans-terrazzo.md`.

## The approach, in plain terms

Instead of a poured or precast terrazzo pan, you set a **tileable base** (custom-sized — e.g. Tile Redi, or a Transolid ready-to-tile) and finish its floor with **terrazzo-look porcelain**. Why porcelain wins on an Eichler slab:

- **Lighter and thinner** than real terrazzo, so it's easier to install over a slab and a sloped pan.
- **No sealing.** Porcelain is non-porous; real terrazzo (cement or epoxy) usually needs periodic sealing.
- **Cheaper and more predictable** than a custom terrazzo pour, and available off the shelf.
- **Consistent color/pattern** you can preview with a sample before committing.

The trade-off: it *looks* like terrazzo rather than being a seamless aggregate slab. On a small Eichler bath floor that difference is minor, and the slip performance is usually better than polished terrazzo.

## The slip question — what actually matters

**The number:** the U.S. standard is **ANSI A326.3**, which sets a minimum **DCOF (dynamic coefficient of friction) of 0.42** for interior *level* floors in wet areas. Treat 0.42 as the floor, not the goal — for a shower pan, higher is better. (On European tile you may instead see **R-ratings** R9–R13, where R10–R11 is typical for wet residential floors, or **barefoot-wet DIN 51097 A/B/C**, where B or C suits a shower.)

**The format matters as much as the number.** For a shower *pan floor* specifically, use **small mosaics** (2"×2", penny round, or hex), not large-format tile, because:

- More grout lines = more traction underfoot (grout breaks up the slick surface; **sanded grout** adds even more grip).
- Small tiles flex to the slope-to-drain; large tiles can't sit flat on a sloped pan.

This is why a tile can meet 0.42 and still not be checked "shower floor" by the maker — a good example is Daltile **Outlander** (below), which is rated for wet/level floors and walls but *not* the shower pan, because it only comes large-format. The clean solution is a **two-part scheme**: a terrazzo-look **mosaic on the pan floor**, and a coordinating **large-format terrazzo-look on the walls and the main bathroom floor**.

## Specific products

### For the shower pan FLOOR (mosaic, slip-appropriate)
- **Daltile Keystones — unglazed porcelain 2"×2" mosaic** (speckle colors like Desert Gray Speckle, Sea Speckle). Unglazed through-body porcelain is the classic slip-safe shower floor; the speckle reads terrazzo-ish, and the small format handles the slope. https://www.daltile.com/mosaic-tile
- **Any terrazzo-look porcelain sold in a 2"×2", penny, or hex mosaic sheet** — several terrazzo collections offer a mosaic in addition to the field tile; ask your tile shop for "the mosaic version of [collection] for a shower floor." Confirm the DCOF ≥ 0.42 (matte, not polished) and use sanded grout.
- **Daltile Poke — 3"×3" speckled ceramic mosaic** is a heavy-speckle terrazzo look, but note Daltile rates it for walls/features, so verify floor DCOF before using it underfoot.

## Before you buy — a short checklist

1. **Pull the spec sheet** for the exact color *and* finish and confirm the **wet DCOF ≥ 0.42** (many collections have a slicker polished version — avoid it on the floor).
2. **Order samples** and wet them; drag a bare foot. Trust the feel plus the number.
3. **Use the mosaic on the pan**, sanded grout, matte finish.
4. **Coordinate, don't match exactly:** a subtle speckled mosaic on the floor with a bolder terrazzo or a single-color tile (green/blue) on the walls reads more mid-century than terrazzo everywhere.
5. This pairs naturally with the **walnut-vanity + one-color** direction in the inspiration board.
