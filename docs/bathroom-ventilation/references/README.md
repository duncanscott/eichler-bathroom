Researched September 22, 2026. The owner confirmed that the tongue-and-groove ceiling is directly beneath a flat roof, with **no attic or open cavity above it**. The owner reported on September 23 that the Panasonic FV-0511VQ1 is already installed. Its projection has not been measured; the roof-side termination and hidden conditions have not been inspected. ## What can be done above the wood ceiling?

**Located curb accessory (links checked September 23, 2026): Fantech 5ACC15FS, item 49580.** Fantech's [commercial accessories catalog](spec-sheets/Fantech-commercial-accessories-curbs.pdf), PDF page 1 (printed page 272), lists this fixed non-vented curb for REC54 / REC6. [HVACQuick's product page](https://hvacquick.com/products/residential/Fans/Exterior-Exhaust-Fans/Fantech-RE-fans) describes a nominal 15-in. curb, 8 in. tall; select **5ACC15FS** in its model table, which also lists the fans. See the [current Fantech roof-curb range](https://www.fantech.net/en-us/products/commercial-ventilation/roof-ventilators/accessory-for-roof-ventilators/curbs/roof-curbs). Confirm the mounting dimensions and roof flashing with the roofer. The catalog's publication date is unconfirmed and its prices are historical; this standard curb does not establish approval for the custom side-entry offset concept below.

**REC54 — direct, short connection:** an intake grille sits against the tongue-and-groove; a sealed duct/transition rises through the roof assembly to the roof fan on a flashed curb. The fan motor is outdoors. The REC54 installation manual actually illustrates a flat-roof curb installation (p. 3). This can preserve the interior ceiling plane. If a longer duct route or silencer is needed, that space must also be provided above the roof in a properly supported and weather-protected installation.

**REC54 — extended/offset connection:** the fan can sit away from the bathroom grille, with a protected duct across the roof. This can provide space for sound control. The proposed sequence is grille → flashed roof penetration → insulated, supported and weather-protected rooftop duct → a designed connection to the fan's bottom inlet inside a raised curb/inlet enclosure. Fantech's manual illustrates remote grilles (p. 3), but its standard curb instructions center the curb over a roof opening (p. 4); the proposed above-roof side-entry enclosure is a custom detail requiring manufacturer and roofer confirmation, not an off-the-shelf detail verified here. The design must establish which roof penetrations are needed rather than assuming a second opening beneath the fan.

An offset route can provide space for sound attenuation and enough duct length to satisfy the **4-ft grille-to-fan duct-length condition** in the remote-fan sound exception discussed below. That means actual duct length, not necessarily 4 ft of horizontal roof separation. It does not guarantee quietness or resolve current certification. Extra bends and duct add resistance; include a weatherproof jacket or enclosure, insulation, compatible supports, sealed joints and condensation management in the proposal. Do not treat ordinary attic flex duct as an exposed outdoor assembly. The site presents the direct, short connection first, followed by the extended/offset route; both sketches are concepts for contractor evaluation.

**Broan concept — ceiling fan inside a raised roof enclosure:** the L100E can discharge directly upward into an 8-in. duct and separate roof termination. Its manual explicitly shows the top-outlet conversion. To retain a flush interior ceiling here, an enclosure above the roof would have to accommodate the 11¾-in.-deep housing, connections and required insulation. The manufacturer drawing is an ordinary ceiling/attic installation; **it does not establish approval for a custom Eichler roof enclosure**. The designer, roofer and fan manufacturer need to agree on that detail.

**Possibly keep the Panasonic:** a sufficiently sized, insulated and weatherproof raised enclosure could potentially contain the already installed housing and its side duct connection, followed by a roof outlet. This is a design option to price, not a documented Panasonic rooftop kit. A smaller enclosure might be possible with the WhisperValue, but it still has a side outlet. Do not tip an indoor fan on its side, cut a new outlet into its housing, or leave it exposed to weather as a workaround.

In all cases, air must discharge **outside**. Neither the roofing layers nor a sealed roof enclosure are an exhaust destination. Duct connections, the ceiling perimeter, insulation/condensation control, drainage, flashing and access for service all belong in the installation detail. [PNNL bathroom exhaust guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans)

## Broan L100E — fan inside a rooftop enclosure

The [site’s Broan section](https://duncanscott.github.io/eichler-bathroom/bathroom-ventilation/#broan) presents the rooftop-enclosure option with a potentially serious compliance issue that remains unresolved. The indoor L100E has a **12¼ × 12¼ × 11¾-in. body**, **14-in. square grille** and **8-in. round outlet**, convertible from side to top. The saved [specification sheet](spec-sheets/Broan-L100E-L300E-specifications.pdf), page 2, gives HVI vertical-discharge ratings of **140 CFM / 0.3 sones at 0.1 in. w.g.** and **101 CFM / 0.6 sones at 0.25**. The headline 130-CFM rating is for horizontal discharge.

With no attic, keeping its grille at the wood ceiling would require protected space above the roof. The proposed air path is **ceiling grille → fan inside a flashed, insulated enclosure → sealed 8-in. metal duct → exterior roof cap**. Exhaust must not discharge into the enclosure. The final enclosure dimensions depend on the roof assembly, support, insulation, connections and service access; 11¾ in. is the fan body depth, not a finished rooftop height. Broan documents the vertical conversion but not this custom enclosure. Confirm the arrangement with Broan and the roofer before fabrication.

The enclosure proposal must also define support for the roof decking and fan, the measured cutout, weatherproof access, roof drainage and the insulation/air barrier. The original grille sits against the ceiling; it does not cover an arbitrary opening. Broan lists room-side installation as “No,” so plan installation access from above and preserve service access to the blower, damper and wiring. The enclosure and roof cap need a measured height and appearance proposal; the fan’s acoustic lining does not establish the required roof insulation.

**On/off operation is permitted.** The [installation manual](spec-sheets/Broan-L100E-L300E-installation.pdf), page 8, explicitly allows an on/off switch or a suitable variable-speed control. Continuous-operation capability does not require continuous running. The model has no built-in humidity sensor; the project’s control requirements remain to be resolved. Above a tub or shower, the manual requires a GFCI-protected branch circuit.

**Unexplained Title 24 entry:** on September 23, 2026, [Broan’s product page](https://broan-nutone.com/en-us/product/ventilationfans/l100e) listed ENERGY STAR certification but marked “Can Be Used To Comply With Title 24” as “No.” The saved documents do not support that entry. The [2025 CEC form](code-reference/California-2025-CF2R-MCH-27-H-ventilation.pdf), page 6, sets the criteria: §7.1 requires airflow and sound ratings from a body accredited to ISO/IEC 17065 applying HVI 920 or equivalent; §7.3.2 caps demand-controlled bathroom fans at 3 sones; §5.2 sets 50 CFM. The [specification sheet](spec-sheets/Broan-L100E-L300E-specifications.pdf) carries HVI-2100 certified ratings of 140 CFM and 0.3 sones vertically at 0.1 in. w.g. Broan’s own multifamily Title 24 sheet states “any Broan fan under 1.0 Sone” as its criterion. The entry is most consistent with a catalog attribute for the whole-dwelling ventilation role rather than demand-controlled local exhaust, but Broan has not confirmed that. Ask Broan in writing, and submit the HVI certified-ratings page with the permit application. The control requirement is separate: the L100E has no built-in humidity sensor, and a humidity control does not by itself answer the Title 24 entry.

## Compact fan cover with exposed custom duct

One indoor layout uses the shallow FV-0510VS1 as follows. Mount the WhisperValue in its normal ceiling orientation inside a compact, supported finished enclosure below the wood. Enclose the fan body, adaptor and necessary connections, with its original **13-in. grille** below. Its body is **10¼ × 10¼ × 3⅜ in.**, four inches shallower than the installed FV-0511VQ1. The factory oval side collar connects to **4-in. duct**. [Product page](https://iaq.na.panasonic.com/ventilation/fans/whispervalue-50-80-100-cfm), [submittal](spec-sheets/Panasonic-FV-0510VS1-submittal.pdf), [installation manual](spec-sheets/Panasonic-FV-0510VS1-installation.pdf).

The duct beyond the local fan cover remains visible. Have a sheet-metal fabricator design a smooth exterior, tidy seams, restrained supports, a deliberate upward bend and neat trim at the roof connection. Coordinate the cover and duct finishes; material and colour are still open. A finish matching the ceiling is an option to sample, not a chosen specification. This is custom construction, not a Panasonic enclosure kit.

**Size the cover and duct independently.** Measure the fan, adaptor, electrical box, support and service space, plus finish and grille projection, to establish the compact cover. Separately show the duct section, transition, lowest elbow point, supports and roof-entry detail. The exposed duct may project below the cover. The body depth alone does not establish the finished cover depth. Make a measured mock-up of both pieces before fabrication; the photo cannot supply exact dimensions.

### Fabrication and performance brief

- Start with the factory outlet and the manufacturer's 4-in. duct arrangement. Round or oval metal duct can be considered for the exposed run. A flatter rectangular section needs its own design review: equal cross-sectional area alone does not establish equal resistance. Do not flatten or reduce the passage solely for appearance.
- Use smooth internal surfaces and appropriately supported, mechanically connected and sealed joints. The finish detail must retain these functions. The Panasonic manual warns against a bend immediately at the adaptor (p. 4) and illustrates **2–3 ft of straight duct before an elbow** (p. 8); measure the available run and assess the complete arrangement.
- Resolve condensation protection and insulation at cold sections and the roof transition. If a visible section needs insulation, show its finished jacket and total outside dimensions as part of the appearance proposal.
- Keep the original grille, motor, damper and electrical connections serviceable. Support the fan using the specified mounting arrangement and suitable framing; preserve the factory housing and damper. Coordinate tub/shower GFCI protection and controls with the installer.
- Verify delivered airflow and assess sound after installation. The selected fan's laboratory sound rating does not predict vibration or airflow noise from the custom duct.

Installation basis: [Panasonic manual](spec-sheets/Panasonic-FV-0510VS1-installation.pdf), [PNNL duct and airflow guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans). The exposed appearance and custom cover are project proposals; final dimensions, transition, material/finish and performance remain to be resolved.

## Elongated enclosure with concealed duct

Another indoor layout puts the fan and sideways duct in **one elongated enclosure**, for either Panasonic model. This provides a continuous finished surface around the equipment and duct. The enclosure must accommodate the fan, duct, elbow, framing, finish and service access. Both indoor layouts use the supplied original grille; an additional matching grille is an optional access detail for the duct compartment. The FV-0510VS1 body is **3⅜ in.** deep versus **7⅜ in.** for the installed FV-0511VQ1. It could permit a shallower and visually lighter enclosure, but the duct, elbow, insulation and finish may set the minimum depth. A four-inch body-depth reduction is not automatically a four-inch reduction in finished drop. Measure how much of the installed fan already projects below the wood before deciding whether replacement is worthwhile.

Both fans specify nominal **13-in. square grilles**, but these are different parts; select the one matching the fan. Encompass showed the VS1 grille out of stock when checked, so confirm delivery before ordering. The service drawings on PDF page 3 of each manual show the grille and springs. Factory springs normally fit slots in the fan housing; a spare grille over a duct-only compartment needs separate supported mounting, with removable access. It is not a manufacturer-provided two-grille soffit kit.

**The second grille is not automatically an exhaust inlet.** In the normal sealed installation, the airflow is bathroom → original grille → fan → sealed duct → outdoors. A second open grille leads only to the cavity around the duct. Passive air exchange can occur as the room dries, but the fan does not intentionally pull air through that cavity. This is an inference from the documented fan arrangement, not a tested Panasonic drying configuration. Forced cavity ventilation would require a deliberately designed, manufacturer-reviewed intake arrangement; do not add holes to the fan casing or exhaust duct.

A spare grille could conceal a **gasketed, removable solid access panel** below the duct, giving a matching appearance while keeping the proposed soffit closed. In that form it is decorative, with no ventilation function. An open spare grille is an alternative to assess for cavity temperature, airflow and drying rather than assuming it prevents mold.

### Moisture details for the compact fan cover

Vents could help a cavity entirely on the room side of the roof's thermal/air boundary exchange air as the bathroom dries. They also admit humid shower air and do not establish a reliable drying airflow; cold surfaces or a leak could still cause wetting. This is an inference from moisture-control principles, not a published Panasonic prohibition on decorative vents.

The current cover concept is **local to the fan, with inspection access and without added side vents**, with the fan flange sealed to the finished underside and a mechanically secured, sealed duct to the outdoors. Keep the compact cover on the indoor side of the roof's insulation and air barrier, seal penetrations, insulate cold duct sections and close the assembly only when dry. The box cavity is outside the exhaust path; it is not an exhaust plenum. Do not block the original grille or manufacturer-required openings. Use suitable timer/humidity control and verify installed airflow. A vented surround remains a design alternative requiring assessment of cavity temperatures, airflow and drying; its vents must not open into the roof assembly. Moisture-resistant finishes do not compensate for leaks. [PNNL installation guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans), [EPA moisture and mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## California and Contra Costa checks that affect selection

The applicable code edition depends on the permit, and the authority may be the city rather than the county. The county's currently linked [bathroom remodel handout](https://www.contracosta.ca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/44647/RESIDENTIAL-BATHROOM-REMODEL) still references **2022 codes**. It describes a 50-CFM minimum, separate fan/light controls, and a retained-ceiling exception. Its download was blocked, so it is linked rather than archived here.

The official [2025 CALGreen residential checklist](code-reference/California-2025-CALGreen-residential-checklist.pdf), effective January 1, 2026, lists ENERGY STAR bathroom fans exhausting outdoors and humidity control unless the fan is part of whole-house ventilation. A separate humidity switch is permitted; adjustable humidity control must cover the specified range up to 80% (p. 12, §4.506.1). A timer alone is not the humidity control described there.

The [2025 CEC ventilation compliance form](code-reference/California-2025-CF2R-MCH-27-H-ventilation.pdf), p. 6, gives a maximum **3 sones for demand-controlled bathroom exhaust** and **1 sone for continuous local exhaust**. Its remote-fan sound exception requires the fan outside the listed interior spaces **and at least 4 ft of duct between fan and grille**. A roof fan immediately above this ceiling may not have that duct length. Have the project team confirm applicability and the actual compliance route rather than assuming that an outdoor motor is automatically exempt.

**The binding question for the REC54 is a certified rating, not ENERGY STAR.** §7.1 of the [2025 CEC form](code-reference/California-2025-CF2R-MCH-27-H-ventilation.pdf), page 6, requires airflow *and* sound ratings from a certification body accredited to ISO/IEC 17065, applying HVI 920 or equivalent. AMCA 210 appears inside that scheme as the airflow test method, so a manufacturer’s own AMCA performance curve is not the certified rating the form asks for. Everything saved here — the 2008 AMCA sheet, the 2010 brochure, the 2007 submittal and the [EPA list dated January 9, 2009](code-reference/ENERGY-STAR-ventilating-fans-historical-list.pdf) — is historical. The §7.3 remote-fan exception waives sound ratings only, so the airflow rating applies on both roof routes regardless of duct length. Ask Fantech whether the REC54 holds a current certified rating. If it does not, a kit sold and listed as a residential bathroom product — Fantech’s PBW110, roughly $243 — keeps the remote-fan layout available, though it is built for wall mounting and a roof arrangement would need Fantech’s confirmation.

The Fantech 2008 sound table also shows that 3.5 sones is not a universal full-speed value: its published fan-sones values are 5.8 at free air, 4.7 at 0.125 in. w.g., 4.1 at 0.25, and 3.5 at 0.5. These remain AMCA free-field values, not directly interchangeable with HVI ceiling-fan sound ratings. Moving the motor outside does not guarantee silence through a short intake duct.

## Items to resolve before choosing a fan

1. Measure the fan/grille and duct/elbow projection below the wood, complete footprint, straight duct length, diameter and framing. Inspect the duct hole and any separate cutout behind the fan. Record roof assembly, beam locations, room area, roof material/slope and exterior termination.
2. Compare and price a compact cover with exposed duct, an elongated enclosure with concealed duct, and an exterior fan with a ceiling grille. Evaluate both Panasonic models for the indoor layouts. Provide comparable measured mock-ups, finish samples, mounting and service details for each option.
3. Require each proposal to show the final roof height/footprint, flashing, insulation, sealed duct/transition, backdraft damper, humidity control, electrical/service access and grille treatment.
4. For REC54, resolve the §7.1 certified-rating question first, then ENERGY STAR evidence and the short-duct sound issue, before purchasing. The 2021 manual calls the 4-in. reducer optional, while the 2010 brochure says supplied; verify the actual package.
5. Select airflow against the installed duct resistance, then measure delivered airflow at the grille. Bathroom size and duct design are not known, so this report does not fix a final CFM setting.
