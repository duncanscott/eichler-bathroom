# Bathroom ventilation through an Eichler roof

Researched September 22, 2026. The owner confirmed that the tongue-and-groove ceiling is directly beneath the roof, with **no attic or open cavity above it**. The owner reported on September 23 that the Panasonic FV-0511VQ1 is already installed. Its projection below the wood and completed duct arrangement have not been measured or inspected here. Actual opening dimensions, roof material/slope, roof assembly thickness, and bathroom dimensions remain unmeasured in this research.

**A dropped ceiling is not the only design route.** The most promising alternative is an exterior roof fan connected to an intake grille at the wood ceiling. A fan with an upward outlet is another possibility, but its housing still needs space: changing the outlet direction does not make the housing fit inside a solid roof assembly.

**First candidate to discuss with the roofer and ventilation installer: Fantech REC54 on a flashed roof curb**, especially if the roof is flat or low slope. This is a physical-fit recommendation, conditional on confirming current certification, sound compliance, airflow, and the roof detail. **Broan L100E is the strongest verified quiet, ENERGY STAR ceiling-fan candidate with a convertible top outlet**, but it is much larger than the Panasonic and would need an approved, weatherproof roof enclosure. Neither is a verified drop-in replacement for this house.

Follow-up: [ceiling grilles for RE54 / REC54](ceiling-grilles.md), including two Fantech round grilles and a larger square aluminum grille that could cover the existing opening with a fabricated transition and mounting frame.

Website: [Bathroom ventilation options](https://duncanscott.github.io/eichler-bathroom/bathroom-ventilation/), prepared for publication through the existing GitHub Pages site. It includes original conceptual illustrations, an appearance comparison, [contractor questions](contractor-brief.txt), and public copies of every downloaded ventilation PDF and related research note. Repository maintenance instructions are in `bathroom-ventilation/site/README.md`.

## What can be done above the wood ceiling?

**Located curb accessory (links checked September 23, 2026): Fantech 5ACC15FS, item 49580.** Fantech's [commercial accessories catalog](spec-sheets/Fantech-commercial-accessories-curbs.pdf), PDF page 1 (printed page 272), lists this fixed non-vented curb for REC54 / REC6. [HVACQuick's product page](https://hvacquick.com/products/residential/Fans/Exterior-Exhaust-Fans/Fantech-RE-fans) describes a nominal 15-in. curb, 8 in. tall; select **5ACC15FS** in its model table, which also lists the fans. See the [current Fantech roof-curb range](https://www.fantech.net/en-us/products/commercial-ventilation/roof-ventilators/accessory-for-roof-ventilators/curbs/roof-curbs). Confirm the mounting dimensions and roof flashing with the roofer. The catalog's publication date is unconfirmed and its prices are historical; this standard curb does not establish approval for the custom side-entry offset concept below.

**Exterior roof fan:** an intake grille sits against the tongue-and-groove; a sealed duct/transition rises through the roof assembly to the roof fan on a flashed curb. The fan motor is outdoors. The REC54 installation manual actually illustrates a flat-roof curb installation (p. 3). This can preserve the interior ceiling plane. If a longer duct route or silencer is needed, that space must also be provided above the roof in a properly supported and weather-protected installation.

**The REC54 need not sit directly above the bathroom grille.** An offset fan with a short duct across the roof is a useful alternative to evaluate, particularly for sound control. The proposed sequence is grille → flashed roof penetration → insulated, supported and weather-protected rooftop duct → a designed connection to the fan's bottom inlet inside a raised curb/inlet enclosure. Fantech's manual illustrates remote grilles (p. 3), but its standard curb instructions center the curb over a roof opening (p. 4); the proposed above-roof side-entry enclosure is a custom detail requiring manufacturer and roofer confirmation, not an off-the-shelf detail verified here. The design must establish which roof penetrations are needed rather than assuming a second opening beneath the fan.

An offset route can provide space for sound attenuation and enough duct length to satisfy the **4-ft grille-to-fan duct-length condition** in the remote-fan sound exception discussed below. That means actual duct length, not necessarily 4 ft of horizontal roof separation. It does not guarantee quietness or resolve current certification. Extra bends and duct add resistance; include a weatherproof jacket or enclosure, insulation, compatible supports, sealed joints and condensation management in the proposal. Do not treat ordinary attic flex duct as an exposed outdoor assembly. The site presents both routes as concepts, with the offset route first for contractor evaluation.

**Ceiling fan inside a raised roof enclosure:** the L100E can discharge directly upward into an 8-in. duct and separate roof termination. Its manual explicitly shows the top-outlet conversion. To retain a flush interior ceiling here, an enclosure above the roof would have to accommodate the 11¾-in.-deep housing, connections and required insulation. The manufacturer drawing is an ordinary ceiling/attic installation; **it does not establish approval for a custom Eichler roof enclosure**. The designer, roofer and fan manufacturer need to agree on that detail.

**Possibly keep the Panasonic:** a sufficiently sized, insulated and weatherproof raised enclosure could potentially contain the already installed housing and its side duct connection, followed by a roof outlet. This is a design option to price, not a documented Panasonic rooftop kit. A smaller enclosure might be possible with the WhisperValue, but it still has a side outlet. Do not tip an indoor fan on its side, cut a new outlet into its housing, or leave it exposed to weather as a workaround.

In all cases, air must discharge **outside**. Neither the roofing layers nor a sealed roof enclosure are an exhaust destination. Duct connections, the ceiling perimeter, insulation/condensation control, drainage, flashing and access for service all belong in the installation detail. [PNNL bathroom exhaust guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans)

## Panasonic FV-0510VS1 in a local soffit below the ceiling

Proposal added September 23, 2026. This is an alternative to an above-roof fan enclosure: mount the WhisperValue in its normal ceiling orientation inside a supported, finished soffit below the wood. Its body is **10¼ × 10¼ × 3⅜ in.**, four inches shallower than the installed FV-0511VQ1. Use the supplied **13-in. grille** in the new underside and the manufacturer's specified brackets. The product's oval side collar connects to **4-in. duct**. [Product page](https://iaq.na.panasonic.com/ventilation/fans/whispervalue-50-80-100-cfm), [submittal](spec-sheets/Panasonic-FV-0510VS1-submittal.pdf), [installation manual](spec-sheets/Panasonic-FV-0510VS1-installation.pdf).

The proposed shape is a narrow strip aligned with a beam or wall, containing the fan and its side duct before the turn toward a flashed roof termination. Panasonic's manual warns against a bend immediately at the adaptor (p. 4) and illustrates **2–3 ft of straight duct before an elbow** (p. 8). Consequently, a small square trim frame around the body alone does not resolve the duct route. The factory housing and damper remain unmodified; this is a custom soffit proposal, not a Panasonic enclosure kit.

For an initial contractor mock-up, allow roughly **16–18 in. width, 48–60 in. length and 6–8 in. finished drop**. These are project planning estimates, not manufacturer-required dimensions or a verified fit. They allow for the grille/frame, fan and adaptor, straight duct, turn, finish and projecting grille. The 4-in. round duct, bend radius, insulation and roof build-up can increase the size. Confirm the entire section and headroom before fabrication; **3⅜ in. is the fan-body depth, not the finished projection**. Reuse the existing ceiling opening only if the geometry permits, otherwise repair/support the decking and restore the roof's insulation and air sealing.

Use bathroom-appropriate wipeable finishes, with removable gasketed inspection access for the duct and electrical connections as needed. Keep the grille removable. Verify the specified mounting method, tub/shower GFCI protection, service access, selected airflow and applicable controls with the installer. The FV-0510VS1 is rated <0.3 / 0.4 / 0.9 sones at 50 / 80 / 100 CFM and 0.1 in. w.g.; the installed FV-0511VQ1 is quieter at 80 CFM under that test condition. Actual installation sound and delivered flow depend on the complete duct arrangement.

### One elongated enclosure and a second matching grille

The fan and sideways duct can share **one elongated enclosure**, for either Panasonic model. The FV-0510VS1 body is **3⅜ in.** deep versus **7⅜ in.** for the installed FV-0511VQ1. It could permit a shallower and visually lighter enclosure, but the duct, elbow, insulation and finish may set the minimum depth. A four-inch body-depth reduction is not automatically a four-inch reduction in finished drop. Measure how much of the installed fan already projects below the wood before deciding whether replacement is worthwhile.

**Matching grilles are sold separately.** Manufacturer service manuals and the Panasonic/Encompass parts catalog identify these exact parts (checked September 23, 2026):

Both fans specify nominal **13-in. square grilles**, but these are different parts; select the one matching the fan. Encompass showed the VS1 grille out of stock when checked, so confirm delivery before ordering. The service drawings on PDF page 3 of each manual show the grille and springs. Factory springs normally fit slots in the fan housing; a spare grille over a duct-only compartment needs separate supported mounting, with removable access. It is not a manufacturer-provided two-grille soffit kit.

**The second grille is not automatically an exhaust inlet.** In the normal sealed installation, the airflow is bathroom → original grille → fan → sealed duct → outdoors. A second open grille leads only to the cavity around the duct. Passive air exchange can occur as the room dries, but the fan does not intentionally pull air through that cavity. This is an inference from the documented fan arrangement, not a tested Panasonic drying configuration. Forced cavity ventilation would require a deliberately designed, manufacturer-reviewed intake arrangement; do not add holes to the fan casing or exhaust duct.

A spare grille could conceal a **gasketed, removable solid access panel** below the duct, giving a matching appearance while keeping the proposed soffit closed. In that form it is decorative, with no ventilation function. An open spare grille is an alternative to assess for cavity temperature, airflow and drying rather than assuming it prevents mold.

### Enclosure vents and moisture

Vents could help a cavity entirely on the room side of the roof's thermal/air boundary exchange air as the bathroom dries. They also admit humid shower air and do not establish a reliable drying airflow; cold surfaces or a leak could still cause wetting. This is an inference from moisture-control principles, not a published Panasonic prohibition on decorative vents.

The starting proposal is a **closed soffit with inspection access, without added side vents**, with the fan flange sealed to the finished underside and a mechanically secured, sealed duct to the outdoors. Keep the soffit on the indoor side of the roof's insulation and air barrier, seal penetrations, insulate cold duct sections and close the assembly only when dry. The box cavity is outside the exhaust path; it is not an exhaust plenum. Do not block the original grille or manufacturer-required openings. Use suitable timer/humidity control and verify installed airflow. A vented surround remains a design alternative requiring assessment of cavity temperatures, airflow and drying; its vents must not open into the roof assembly. Moisture-resistant finishes do not compensate for leaks. [PNNL installation guidance](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/bathroom-exhaust-fans), [EPA moisture and mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## Effect on the existing hole

The FV-0511VQ1 installation manual shows a **10½ × 10½ ceiling opening for new construction** (p. 9, Fig. 7) and **10⅞ × 10⅞ for retrofit** (p. 10, Fig. 10). Neither is a measurement of the hole already cut. Its 10¼-in. housing and 13-in. grille are different dimensions. The grille fit comparisons below use 10½ in. as an assumption; measure the actual opening before fabrication.

| Approach | Expected treatment of opening |
|---|---|
| Retain FV-0511VQ1 | Measure its installed position. Retaining a flush grille requires protected space above the wood; a below-ceiling soffit may change the fan position and use of the opening. |
| Use FV-0510VS1 | For a flush installation, nominal housing width/length and grille size match; verify mounting. For the below-ceiling soffit, reuse the existing opening only if the duct route fits, otherwise repair/support and seal it. |
| Use L100E | Its 12¼-in. housing is larger than a 10½-in. opening. Enlargement/support and the top duct require planning; its 14-in. grille is not the rough-opening size. |
| Use RE54/REC54 | A 4–5-in. duct does not fill the existing square hole. Use a properly sealed transition/plenum and an adequately sized intake grille/trim, or repair the surrounding decking. Select the grille by free area and pressure loss as well as appearance. |

Because the wood may also be structural roof decking, establish support for cut boards and locate beams before enlarging anything. The saved dimensions are selection information, not a roof-cutting plan.

## California and Contra Costa checks that affect selection

The applicable code edition depends on the permit, and the authority may be the city rather than the county. The county's currently linked [bathroom remodel handout](https://www.contracosta.ca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/44647/RESIDENTIAL-BATHROOM-REMODEL) still references **2022 codes**. It describes a 50-CFM minimum, separate fan/light controls, and a retained-ceiling exception. Its download was blocked, so it is linked rather than archived here.

The official [2025 CALGreen residential checklist](code-reference/California-2025-CALGreen-residential-checklist.pdf), effective January 1, 2026, lists ENERGY STAR bathroom fans exhausting outdoors and humidity control unless the fan is part of whole-house ventilation. A separate humidity switch is permitted; adjustable humidity control must cover the specified range up to 80% (p. 12, §4.506.1). A timer alone is not the humidity control described there.

The [2025 CEC ventilation compliance form](code-reference/California-2025-CF2R-MCH-27-H-ventilation.pdf), p. 6, gives a maximum **3 sones for demand-controlled bathroom exhaust** and **1 sone for continuous local exhaust**. Its remote-fan sound exception requires the fan outside the listed interior spaces **and at least 4 ft of duct between fan and grille**. A roof fan immediately above this ceiling may not have that duct length. Have the project team confirm applicability and the actual compliance route rather than assuming that an outdoor motor is automatically exempt.

**Fantech certification evidence is historical.** The saved 2007 manufacturer submittal marks RE54/REC54 as ENERGY STAR qualified; an [official EPA list dated January 9, 2009](code-reference/ENERGY-STAR-ventilating-fans-historical-list.pdf) also lists them. Current distributor listings repeat that claim. I did not obtain a current certification record for the unit that would ship. This is not evidence of noncompliance, but it is not enough to present it as a fully verified California purchase. Ask Fantech for the current model/item-specific certification and sound data for the proposed arrangement.

The Fantech 2008 sound table also shows that 3.5 sones is not a universal full-speed value: its published fan-sones values are 5.8 at free air, 4.7 at 0.125 in. w.g., 4.1 at 0.25, and 3.5 at 0.5. These remain AMCA free-field values, not directly interchangeable with HVI ceiling-fan sound ratings. Moving the motor outside does not guarantee silence through a short intake duct.

## Items to resolve before choosing a fan

1. Measure the existing opening, tongue-and-groove thickness, roof assembly, beam locations and bathroom floor area. Identify roof material and slope.
2. Compare REC54 with roof curb and ceiling grille against an elongated indoor soffit for the installed FV-0511VQ1 or shallower FV-0510VS1. If retaining a flush ceiling grille is preferred, separately price a manufacturer-reviewed raised roof enclosure.
3. Require each proposal to show the final roof height/footprint, flashing, insulation, sealed duct/transition, backdraft damper, humidity control, electrical/service access and grille treatment.
4. For RE54/REC54, resolve current ENERGY STAR evidence and the short-duct sound issue before purchasing. The 2021 manual calls the 4-in. reducer optional, while the 2010 brochure says supplied; verify the actual package.
5. Select airflow against the installed duct resistance, then measure delivered airflow at the grille. Bathroom size and duct design are not known, so this report does not fix a final CFM setting.
