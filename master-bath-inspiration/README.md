## Strongest direction for an Eichler

A convincing Eichler primary bath does not need to look like a period set. The best references combine a few era-appropriate moves with current performance:

- Use a simple floating walnut or rift-cut white-oak vanity, preferably furniture-like rather than heavily paneled.
- Choose one expressive tile gesture: saturated 2x8 or 4x4 field tile, an atomic/geometric accent, or restrained terrazzo. Avoid stacking several competing retro motifs.
- Keep the ceiling, skylight, clerestory, or garden view visually important. Natural light and indoor-outdoor connection carry more Eichler character than decorative accessories.
- Favor globe or compact geometric sconces, thin mirrors or medicine cabinets, and quiet chrome, black, or aged-brass fixtures.
- Keep the wet area visually open with frameless glass or a single clear partition.
- Let warm wood and one period color do most of the work; keep countertops and the remaining surfaces calm.

Three coherent schemes emerge from the references:

1. **Walnut + blue/green tile** — the most recognizably Eichler and the best all-around direction. See 01, 08, 11, 14, and 16.
2. **Walnut + terrazzo + white** — brighter and more contemporary while still grounded in the period. See 01, 02, 06, 15, and 18.
3. **Indoor-outdoor spa** — strongest when the room already has a skylight, clerestory, atrium edge, or private garden exposure. See 07, 10, 12, 17, and 19.

## Eichler-specific cautions

- Treat fixture relocation as a major decision. In a slab-on-grade Eichler, moving drains or supply lines can require opening the slab near radiant-heating lines. [Eichler Network discussion](https://www.eichlernetwork.com/content/bathroom-remodel)
- Before demolition, map and test radiant heat, domestic water, and sewer lines. [Eichler Network overview](https://www.eichlernetwork.com/article/bathrooms-rebound)
- Preserve useful original geometry when possible. A better vanity, storage plan, lighting, and glass enclosure can transform a small footprint without chasing a larger conventional primary suite.
- Confirm waterproofing, ventilation, tempered-glass, electrical, and accessibility/code requirements with the project team. The saved references are design inspiration, not construction details.
