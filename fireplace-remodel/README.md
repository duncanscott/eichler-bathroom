# Tiling an Eichler Fireplace

Research report · September 10, 2026

The question is whether an Eichler fireplace can retain a convincing architectural presence when its original brick is covered with tile, potentially concealing surface damage. Six documented Eichler examples (sections 1 to 6) provide visual references, followed by adjacent mid-century work by the same designer and a set of Pinterest leads. One source explicitly describes tile over original brick; another supplies a painted-brick-to-tile before-and-after pair.

This collection records design options, not an approved remodel or a diagnosis of this project's fireplace. No photograph, damage assessment, dimensions, material selection, or installation specification for our own fireplace has been supplied.

## The three references

| Reference | Appearance | What is documented | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunnyvale / Urbanism Designs | White wave-textured tile | Painted masonry before; tiled face and removal of the raised hearth afterward | Exact tile model, substrate preparation, and original masonry condition |
| 8180 Phaeton Drive, Oakland | Gray hexagonal tile with pale grout | Tiled face, returns, and hearth in an identified Eichler | Tile maker, installation assembly, and condition beneath the finish |
| Destination Eichler's own home | Beige stone tile; subsequently restored to brick | The owner explicitly says original brick had been covered with tile | Why the earlier owner covered it; whether damage played any role |

**Evidence boundary:** none of the sources establishes that concealing brick damage was the reason for tiling. They document appearance and, in two cases, a remodeling sequence. They do not establish an appropriate repair method for our fireplace.

## 1. Sunnyvale: white wave-textured tile

### Finished fireplace

![Sunnyvale Eichler fireplace refaced in white wave-textured tile](images/01-sunnyvale-white-wave-tile.jpg)

### Before refacing

![The same Sunnyvale fireplace with painted masonry and a raised hearth](images/04-sunnyvale-before-refacing.jpg)

**Published remodel account, paraphrased:** Pamela Lin and Erwin Tam's Sunnyvale Eichler had a previously painted fireplace. They were concerned about the raised hearth as a trip hazard for their children. The remodel removed it and introduced wavy Porcelanosa tile. The room was arranged for family use and piano playing. The article includes both photographs above. [Houzz: *An Eichler Remodel Spawns a Design Career*, Mitchell Parker, December 5, 2013](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/houzz-tour-an-eichler-remodel-spawns-a-design-career-stsetivw-vs~20757925).

**Our design reading:** the predominantly white surface keeps the fireplace visually quiet while adding texture. The dark opening remains the focal point. This was the assistant's initial aesthetic preference; the user has not selected it.

The hearth alteration is part of this precedent, not a recommendation to reproduce that detail in our house.

## 2. Oakland: gray hexagonal tile

![Gray hexagonal tile on the fireplace face, sides and hearth at 8180 Phaeton Drive](images/02-oakland-gray-hexagon-tile.jpg)

**Published account, paraphrased:** Dwell identifies this house as 8180 Phaeton Drive in Oakland's Sequoyah Hills, a 1965 Eichler designed by Claude Oakland. The article dates the house renovation to 2016 and describes the fireplace as tiled. It does not provide an owner interview or a fireplace construction narrative. [Dwell: *A Sunny, Updated Eichler in Oakland Just Listed for $1.3M*, Jennifer Baum Lagdameo, August 7, 2020](https://www.dwell.com/article/sequoyah-hills-eichler-home-claude-oakland-real-estate-d284c6f4).

**Our design reading:** the hexagons create a much stronger graphic feature than the Sunnyvale treatment. The finish wraps around the sides and continues onto the hearth, making it useful for considering the whole fireplace volume rather than the front face alone. The photographed finish conceals the masonry pattern; the underlying assembly is unverified.

The linked article is a historical listing feature. Its asking price and availability are not current research findings.

## 3. Destination Eichler: tile over brick, then restoration

### A. Before: beige tile conceals the brick

![Destination Eichler's fireplace covered in beige stone tile before restoration](images/03-destination-eichler-tile-before.jpg)

### B. After tile removal

![Brick exposed after tile removal, with substantial surface residue](images/05-destination-eichler-brick-after-tile-removal.jpg)

### C. After professional cleaning

![The brick fireplace after professional cleaning](images/06-destination-eichler-brick-after-cleaning.webp)

**Published restoration account, paraphrased:** Karen Nepacena describes original brick covered by beige stone tile. After removal, a stubborn coating resisted paint remover and a small soda-blasting setup. The owners hired professionals, who used soda with glass bead and completed the cleaning in approximately four hours. Some residual plaster still needed attention. [Destination Eichler: *Stripping Down: Restoring a Fireplace*](https://www.destinationeichler.com/blog/restoring-a-brick-fireplace).

This is a record of what those owners did, not a cleaning specification for our brick.

### D. Restored fireplace and surrounding room

![Restored brick fireplace with clear side glazing and walnut paneling](images/07-destination-eichler-restored-fireplace.jpg)

**Published follow-up, paraphrased:** the owners replaced the non-original side glazing with clear glass and completed additional walnut paneling. The finished room restored the visual relationship between the chimney, glazing, and interior. The post still mentioned small areas of cleanup. [Destination Eichler: *Dining Room and Fireplace Reveal*](https://www.destinationeichler.com/blog/dining-room-and-fireplace-reveal).

The source pages display February 1 and February 13 respectively; no publication year is asserted here because the visible article date headings omit it.

**Our design reading:** this sequence makes both choices tangible: a completely covered fireplace and the brick revealed beneath it. It also shows that returning to exposed brick involved more than lifting off the tile. That matters if future restoration is part of the decision.

## 4. San Jose: glossy blue glazed thin brick (Destination Eichler)

![Destination Eichler San Jose fireplace re-clad in Aleutian glazed thin brick](images/08-san-jose-destination-eichler-aleutian-glazed-thin-brick.jpg)

**Published account, paraphrased:** Fireclay Tile's gallery entry credits the design to Destination Eichler and the photo to Christopher Dibble, places the house in San Jose, and describes the fireplace as "creating a dialogue with the home's Midcentury Modern architecture." The blue Aleutian colour has been discontinued; Fireclay suggests Appalachian as the nearest match. Fireclay's floor-to-ceiling article adds that the thin brick is "substantially lighter than traditional brick" and must stay outside the firebox. [Fireclay Tile: *Destination Eichler: Glazed Thin Brick Fireplace*](https://www.fireclaytile.com/gallery/detail/destination-eichler-aleutian-fireplace) · [Fireclay blog: *Make a Statement with a Floor to Ceiling Tile Fireplace*](https://www.fireclaytile.com/blogs/fireclay-tile-blog/make-a-statement-with-a-floor-to-ceiling-tile-fireplace).

**Our design reading:** this is the strongest precedent for the question asked. The whole masonry mass is re-clad in a brick-format tile, so the original brick and any damage disappear while the fireplace keeps a masonry character, coursing and proportion. Straight-set 2.5×8 brick tile echoes the Fireclay 4×8 straight-set precedent already recorded for the bathroom in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md). Whether the fireplace was damaged before cladding is not stated.

## 5. Bay Area: handpainted Starburst surround (Destination Eichler)

![Destination Eichler fireplace surround in handpainted Starburst tile](images/09-bay-area-destination-eichler-handpainted-starburst-surround.jpg)

**Published account, paraphrased:** Fireclay describes 8×8 handpainted Starburst tile "taking cues from Midcentury Modern motifs" applied to the surround of a Bay Area home, design and photo by Destination Eichler. [Fireclay Tile: *Destination Eichler: Handpainted Fireplace Tiles*](https://www.fireclaytile.com/gallery/detail/destination-eichler-starburst-fireplace).

**Our design reading:** the opposite strategy from section 4. Only the surround is tiled; the body stays plain and a shelf survives. Useful if the damage is local to the opening. The photograph is low resolution and the house is not named as an Eichler on the page, only in the project title.

## 6. Eichler refaced in Arto concrete tile (JRP Design & Remodel)

![Eichler fireplace refaced in Frank Lloyd Wright-inspired Arto tile, wide view](images/10-atomic-ranch-jrp-arto-tile-fireplace-wide.jpg)

![The same Arto-tile fireplace seen from the side against the glass wall](images/11-atomic-ranch-jrp-arto-tile-fireplace-tall.jpg)

**Published account, paraphrased:** Atomic Ranch's house tour of a heavily renovated Eichler by Lori and Justin of JRP Design & Remodel says "Lori chose to reface the fireplace in a Frank Lloyd Wright-inspired Arto Tile, which adds a lovely, rich central feature to the room." The image alt text calls it a concrete tile surround. [Atomic Ranch: *Inside an Epic Eichler Renovation*](https://www.atomic-ranch.com/architecture-design/house-tours/inside-an-epic-eichler-renovation/).

**Our design reading:** large-format relief tile turns the chimney into one sculptural object and hides the masonry completely. It reads more 1920s Wright than 1960s Eichler, which is a taste decision rather than a fault; the pale, matte colour keeps it from dominating the room.

![Handpainted Fallow tile with wood slats on the family-room face](images/14-adjacent-pleasanton-ranch-fallow-handpainted.jpg)

The living-room side is stacked 2.5×11.5 Norman glazed thin brick in matte Black Hills "rising gracefully to the angled ceiling"; the other side is 8×8 handpainted Fallow tile in White Motif with vertical wood slats. A further adjacent example from the same Fireclay article: a freestanding mid-century fireplace (design and photo Claire Thomas) re-clad in red glazed thin brick, showing that brick-format tile can keep the original red-brick character rather than replace it. ## What the owner community says

The forum threads, designer interviews and blog posts that discuss covering an Eichler fireplace are re-created, with links and verbatim quotes, in [owner-discussions.md](owner-discussions.md). In short: tiling over brick is treated as legitimate by owners, by an Eichler architect (John Klopf) and by an Eichler-specialist designer (Karen Nepacena); the objections are to the wrong material, mainly travertine, beige stone and faux-aged plaster; tile must be set in mortar or thin-set and bonds into the brick, so it is close to a one-way decision; moisture or staining must be fixed before it is covered; and where the chimney passes through glass, inside and outside faces should read as one material.

## Questions for the next design discussion

- What exactly do we want to conceal: discoloration, paint, missing brick faces, old repairs, or something else?
- Do we prefer a quiet textured plane, a graphic tile pattern, or the appearance of exposed masonry?
- How should the finish meet the firebox opening, hearth, ceiling, and adjacent glass?
- Should the inside and outside of the chimney read as the same material where they are visible together?
- Is preserving the possibility of future brick restoration a priority?

These are open questions, not recorded decisions. The immediate useful next input would be clear photographs of our fireplace, its sides and hearth, and close views of the affected brick.
