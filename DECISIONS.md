# Decisions — Eichler Primary Bath Remodel

Running record of what's been *decided*, as opposed to the research in `master-bath-inspiration/` and `master-bath-inspiration-2/`, which deliberately presents options. Contractors and future readers should treat anything here as settled unless it says otherwise.

Last updated: August 9, 2026.

---

## Floor finish — DECIDED: terrazzo or terrazzo look-alike

**Decision (2026-08-09):** the bathroom floor and shower base direction is **real terrazzo or a terrazzo look-alike.** Please do not bid alternative floor aesthetics unless asked.

**Considered and set aside:**

- **VCT (vinyl composition tile).** This is the period-correct Eichler floor — the original houses used 9×9 vinyl-asbestos tile (VAT), of which modern VCT is the asbestos-free descendant — and it's cheap, thin, and excellent over a radiant slab. Set aside anyway: the seams aren't waterproof, it needs an ongoing strip-and-wax regime, waxed VCT is slippery when wet, and it must never go inside a shower pan.
- **Plain (non-terrazzo) porcelain and solid surface in solid colors.** Fine materials, wrong look for this house.

**Preferred execution, in order:**

1. **One-piece custom cast base** — epoxy terrazzo (Angelozzi TERRALITE) or terrazzo-pattern solid surface (Grifform Corian/HI-MACS). No grout joints; the monolithic plane is the most architecturally Eichler-correct result. See `master-bath-inspiration-2/custom-terrazzo-shower-bases.md`.
2. **Custom tileable pan finished with terrazzo** — e.g. Tile Redi "Redi Your Way," finished with honed small-format real terrazzo or slip-rated terrazzo-look porcelain mosaic. See `master-bath-inspiration-2/shower-floor-terrazzo-porcelain.md`.
3. **Stock pre-tiled base** — only if the rough opening happens to match a stock size. See `master-bath-inspiration-2/shower-pans-terrazzo.md`.

**Non-negotiable constraint:** whatever goes on the shower floor must have a tested wet **DCOF ≥ 0.42** (ANSI A326.3), and higher is better. Polished terrazzo is not acceptable underfoot — honed, matte, or textured only. If tiled rather than cast, use small-format mosaic with sanded grout so it follows the slope and adds traction.

**Aesthetic:** warm light base (off-white or pale warm gray) with *sparse* charcoal, rust, muted green, and cream aggregate. Deliberately not the busy "confetti terrazzo" currently in fashion. Matte/honed finish, discreet linear or color-matched drain.

## Still open

- **Rough opening dimensions and existing drain location** — must be measured stud-to-stud before any custom base can be ordered. This is the current blocker on pricing.
- **Curbless vs. curbed entry** — curbless requires recessing the slab; decide early, it drives demo and framing.
- **Vanity** — direction is floating wood with flat slab fronts, continuous grain, and small round recessed pulls; species and maker not chosen. Note that the Lancashire reference house used custom *bamboo*, not walnut, for exactly this look.
- **Wall tile colour** — leaning to one saturated period colour (green or blue) against an otherwise calm envelope; not selected.

## Standing constraints for any bid

- The house is **slab-on-grade**. Relocating drains or supply lines can mean opening the slab near radiant-heating lines — price accordingly, and prefer solutions that keep the existing drain location.
- **Map and test radiant heat, water, and sewer lines before demolition.**
- Any original 9×9 floor tile and its black mastic should be **assumed to contain asbestos until tested.** Intact tile is not hazardous; breaking, sanding, or scraping it is. Test first, then either encapsulate or use licensed abatement.
- Post-and-beam framing limits attic and crawl access for new duct and wiring runs.
